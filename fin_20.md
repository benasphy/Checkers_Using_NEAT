**5. Why Elo Is Unstable**
The apparent rise and fall is not one phenomenon.

- **The historical phenotype changes discontinuously as topology mutates.** A network can become effectively constant when an orphan incoming edge prevents output scheduling. This makes topology mutation look like catastrophic strength loss even when the underlying genes still contain an input-output path.
- **Each probe evaluates a different generation champion.** The plotted sequence is not a monotonic archive and not necessarily one lineage.
- **The generation champion is selected with extremely noisy fitness.** Selecting the maximum among 96 estimates amplifies positive noise.
- **Current default probe size is eight games per rung.** For a 50 percent score, the binomial standard error is about 0.177 before accounting for opening or opponent correlations.
- **Probe game conditions change with generation.** `base_seed=(seed << 8) ^ (gen * 131 + i)` at `ai/train_path_a.py:274` means generations are not evaluated on a common matched game set.
- **When material-rung scores are all zero, Elo is largely determined by the random rung.** Small changes against random then produce large apparent rating swings.
- **Repeatedly taking the best observed Elo creates selection bias.** With 60 path-A probes, even a stationary player will eventually produce an unusually high estimate.
- **The coevolutionary objective itself changes.** Peer populations and HOF composition vary every generation.
- **The ladder calibration changes across run families.** Some artifacts use pure material, others appear to use an undocumented positional variant while retaining the same rung names.
- **A scalar Elo model can be poor for nontransitive agents.** Different neural evaluators may have rock-paper-scissors relationships against heuristic opponents.

The observed path-A pattern, Elo `277` at generation 115 followed by `66` at generation 295, does not establish genuine regression. It is compatible with a constant or weak agent plus phenotype failures, probe noise, and winner's curse. Genuine forgetting should only be assessed after correctness fixes using the same frozen opening/opponent suite at every checkpoint.

**6. Why Fitness And Elo Are Misaligned**
The metrics answer different questions:

| Metric | Actual question |
|---|---|
| Training fitness | How did this genome score against a small, changing sample of current peers and HOF members, with material-shaped draws? |
| Probe Elo | What scalar rating best matches this champion's score against a small fixed-opponent family on this generation's sampled games? |
| True objective | How strong and robust is the final agent against independent opponents and unseen openings under a fixed protocol? |

Specific causes:

- Peer games are close to zero-sum, so population mean fitness naturally remains near 0.5.
- A high relative fitness can occur while the whole population is weak.
- Random HOF assignment makes fitness values incomparable even within a generation because opponents differ in strength.
- Fitness is estimated from too few games to rank similar genomes reliably.
- The best fitness of 96 noisy estimates is expected to be extreme.
- HOF gate scores do not provide an absolute anchor because the archive itself derives from the same weak ecology.
- Material-shaped draws reward an intermediate proxy, while Elo uses strict points.
- `neat.Population.best_genome` compares raw fitness across generations even though the opponent distribution changes; installed `neat/population.py:98-100`.
- At training completion, `winner` can overwrite `best_value_genome.pkl`; `ai/train_path_a.py:303-311`.
- The network output is described as expected outcome, but it is not trained on value targets or calibrated probabilities. It is an evolved search heuristic.

The empirical evidence is decisive: fitness/Elo correlations are approximately zero in the two long pilots. Optimizing current training fitness is therefore not a reliable route to stronger independent play.

**7. Material D1 D2 D4**
The complete active path is:

1. `LADDER` defines `("material", depth)` opponents at `ai/ladder.py:77-84`.
2. `make_agent()` wraps `material_eval` in `Searcher`; `ai/ladder.py:57-69`.

Therefore, in the validation records, `0.000` means the candidate obtained zero strict points in those games. The exact W/D/L reconciliation rules out a final aggregation or perspective inversion.

The root cause is a combination:

- **Historical neural evaluations were often constant zero.** Search then used terminal detections and forced-capture continuation but had no meaningful quiet-position heuristic.
- **A zero-evaluation depth-4 player can still beat random sometimes.** Terminal tactics and mandatory captures supply limited competence.
- **Even shallow material search is much stronger than random under this engine.** `test_material_d2_crushes_random()` requires at least 6/8 points; `tests/test_ai.py:69-73`.
- **Probe samples are too small to distinguish a 1-3 percent score from zero.**
- **The rungs are not ordered reliably by nominal depth.** Pure material exhibits horizon/game-tree pathology. A d4 opponent being weaker than d2 is not itself an Elo arithmetic bug; it means depth is not a valid strength label for this heuristic.
- **Historical "v2" rungs appear to use positional terms absent from current source.** Results with identical `material-dN` names are not semantically comparable.
- **The current custom compiler changes activation semantics.** Post-Aug-19 results cannot be compared directly with pre-Aug-19 runs.

Root-cause verdict: **the zeros are real match outcomes generated by a weak or broken candidate, not a final score-normalization bug.** The candidate weakness was primarily implementation-induced, not evidence that NEAT cannot learn Checkers.

- **Calibration games:** Current `calibrate_ladder()` plays every pair among random, d1, d2, and d4, with 40 games per pair by default; `ai/train_path_a.py:48-72`.
- **Opponent stationarity:** Opponent code is fixed within one source version, but calibration caches contain only ratings. A code change can silently reuse stale ratings.
- **Cross-run comparability:** Current training stores calibration under each run directory and seeds calibration with `seed + 7`; `ai/train_path_a.py:165-167`. Different cells can therefore use different scales.
- **Colors:** `pair_tasks()` alternates colors, which balances counts for even game totals; `ai/arena.py:68-80`.
- **Paired conditions:** The two color games use different game seeds, so they are not exact matched opening/tie-break pairs.
- **Odd game totals:** The API does not enforce an even game count despite claiming color balance.
- **Opening diversity:** Active source always starts at the initial position. Agent seed affects root tie-breaking, not a controlled opening suite.
- **Determinism:** Game agents are seeded, but training evolution is not globally seeded.
- **Probe size:** Current default is eight games per rung; `ai/train_path_a.py:113-117`.
- **Performance rating:** `performance_rating()` aggregates all opponent points, clamps perfect/zero scores to 0.25 from the boundary, then solves one rating; `ai/train_path_a.py:75-93`. The floor/ceiling therefore depends on game count.
- **Uncertainty:** No confidence interval is computed for calibration or per-generation performance rating.
- **Model fit:** No residual or transitivity test checks whether one Elo dimension describes the opponent graph.
- **Color advantage:** Elo fitting has no first-player parameter.
- **Game dependence:** Color pairs and repeated openings are treated as independent observations.
- **Checkpoint selection:** The highest observed probe Elo is retained, introducing multiple-testing bias.
- **Final evaluation:** `final_eval.py` loads that best-Elo checkpoint and evaluates the same ladder family; `experiments/final_eval.py:72-92`.
- **Current executability:** `final_eval.py` is currently broken by its unsupported `opening_plies` argument.
- **External meaning:** Random=0 is arbitrary. A value such as 700 is not comparable to human ratings, CheckerBoard engine ratings, or tournament Elo.

A non-monotonic material-depth ladder is not automatically an Elo arithmetic failure. Elo can assign d4 below d2 if d4 genuinely performs worse. The real failures are assuming nominal depth means strength, not testing model fit/nontransitivity, and changing opponent semantics while preserving labels.

Stable protocol:

1. Version every opponent by exact evaluator, search implementation, node budget, draw rules, and source hash.
2. Generate and freeze a test opening suite before training.
3. Play every opening with colors swapped under identical opening conditions.
4. Store raw game records, not only aggregate ratings.
5. Use one project-wide calibration dataset and preserve it unchanged.
6. Fit ratings jointly with a color term and bootstrap over opening pairs.
7. Report per-opponent W/D/L and score alongside any rating.
8. Select checkpoints on a separate validation suite.
9. Evaluate the locked final checkpoint exactly once on held-out test opponents/openings.
10. Call the result "internal performance rating" unless linked to an external rating pool.

**10. Fitness Audit**
Current objective:

\[
f(g)=\frac{1}{N_g}\sum_i s_i
\]

where wins/losses are 1/0 and drawn games become:

\[
0.5 + 0.1\tanh(\text{material}/300)
\]

This is not outcome-only fitness. It is outcome-dominant shaped fitness.

Problems:

- Four peer games plus two HOF games are insufficient to rank 96 similar genomes.
- Different genomes face different HOF members.
- Peer score is relative, so population-wide improvement need not alter mean fitness.
- HOF quality derives from the same ecology and does not create an independent absolute target.
- Material shaping can be optimized without improving strict win rate.
- Material adjudication turns some capped games into wins, further coupling fitness to handcrafted material.
- Search depth means genome fitness measures a search-plus-network system, not network quality alone.
- The opponent distribution changes by generation.
- Fitness values from different generations are not on the same scale.
- NEAT stagnation uses species maximum, compounding winner's curse.

Recommended objective design:

- Use strict W/D/L as the primary fitness signal.
- Use a common opponent panel for all genomes in a generation.
- Keep the panel as a mixture of peers, immutable historical training anchors, and separate non-test heuristic anchors.
- Use exact paired openings and color swaps.
- Allocate a small initial game count to all genomes, then use adaptive racing for uncertain top/survival-boundary genomes.
- If shaped draw material is retained, preregister it as a separate condition and ablate it against strict outcomes.
- Do not train directly on the test Elo or final test ladder.
- Preserve raw score vectors rather than collapsing every opponent to one number.
- Estimate fitness uncertainty and avoid treating tiny differences as exact rankings.

A practical, low-compute primary condition is strict score against shared peers plus a small immutable training archive. A separate ablation can test whether bounded material shaping improves sample efficiency without harming held-out strict strength.

**11. Methodology Weaknesses**
| Weakness | Consequence | Required correction |
|---|---|---|
| Missing source version for historical runs | Results cannot be reproduced or audited exactly | Commit source/configs before running and save manifest with every cell |
| Only incomplete condition/seed coverage | No treatment-effect inference | At least 5 independent seeds; 10 preferred |
| Linear fixed baseline | Straw comparison conflates topology and capacity | Add capacity-matched nonlinear fixed architecture |
| Same game count called equal compute | Deeper search and larger networks cost more | Report and control games, search nodes, network operations, and wall time |
| Probe ladder used for checkpoint selection and reporting | Optimistic test leakage | Separate validation and test suites |
| Changing evaluation seeds by generation | No paired longitudinal comparison | Reuse one frozen validation opening suite |
| No raw game dataset | Cannot audit Elo, adjudication, color, or openings | Write one record per game with full metadata |
| Normal 1.96 CI at very small seed count | Understates uncertainty | Use seed-level paired intervals and hierarchical bootstrap |
| Games treated as independent | Pseudoreplication | Seed is the independent training unit; opening pairs are clustered observations |
| Smoothed means with pointwise unsmoothed CIs | Misleading learning-curve graphics | Show raw seed curves and bootstrap bands for predefined summaries |
| Best of many generations reported | Multiple-comparison bias | Predefine final generation or select on independent validation |
| No external opponent | Internal Elo cannot support high-strength claim | Integrate a fixed external engine or standardized test suite |
| No official opening protocol | Repeated-start overfitting and low effective sample size | Use a versioned, disjoint opening suite, ideally compatible with American Checkers practice |
| Unpinned dependencies/defaults | Behavior changes across machines | Lock environment and make NEAT defaults explicit |
| Checkpoint state split across files | Resume can create hybrid state | Transactional checkpoint containing all run state |
| No formal power analysis | Risk of expensive underpowered experiment | Estimate seed variance from corrected pilot before full study |

**12. Best Research Question**
The original question is salvageable but currently too weak because the fixed condition is linear and compute is not matched.

Recommended primary question:

> **Under a fixed game and search-compute budget, does evolving neural topology improve the held-out playing strength and sample efficiency of neuroevolved Checkers value functions relative to capacity-matched fixed topologies?**

Recommended title:

> **Does Topology Help Under a Fixed Search Budget? A Capacity-Matched Study of Neuroevolved Checkers Value Functions**

Primary hypothesis:

> Capacity-matched topology evolution will improve either final held-out strength or strength-per-game by discovering useful sparse nonlinear feature interactions that a fixed architecture cannot exploit as efficiently.

Null hypothesis:

> After matching active parameter count and compute, evolving topology provides no meaningful advantage over fixed-topology weight evolution.

Useful secondary question:

> Does topology evolution improve robustness across unseen openings and search budgets, or only optimize the training ecology?

Do not make MCTS, reinforcement learning, or policy networks part of the primary paper now. They add confounds and compute before the current value-search pipeline is trustworthy. A future paper could compare alpha-beta and MCTS after the topology question is answered.

Novelty assessment:

- Fixed versus evolving topology alone has moderate novelty because Blondie24 and HyperNEAT-related Checkers work already exist.
- Capacity matching, fixed search budgets, independent strength evaluation, and a resource-constrained setting make the study substantially stronger.
- A rigorously controlled negative result is publishable if the methodology is sound.
- The phenotype failure is valuable engineering evidence but should not be the paper's central scientific contribution.

**13. Experimental Design**
Primary trained conditions:

| Condition | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Fixed-linear | Diagnostic continuation of the current `36 → 1` control |
| Fixed-nonlinear | Primary fixed-topology baseline, matched to NEAT's preregistered active-parameter budget |
| NEAT-topology | Structural and parametric mutation under the same maximum active-parameter budget |

Non-trained reference opponents:

| Opponent | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Uniform random | Plumbing and floor sanity check |
| Greedy material | Minimal tactical/material baseline |
| Positional alpha-beta | Stronger handcrafted baseline |
| Multiple fixed search-node budgets | Strength curve rather than misleading nominal depth labels |
| Frozen corrected pilot champions | Longitudinal/nontransitivity diagnostics, not final external evidence |
| External fixed engine or standardized suite | Required before claiming high or externally meaningful strength |
| Small endgame tablebase tests, if feasible | Objective endgame correctness and conversion testing |

Controls:

- Same training opening distribution.
- Same exact opening positions per paired condition/seed.
- Both colors from every opening.
- Same game budget per genome.
- Same search-node budget per move rather than only nominal depth.
- Same maximum active network parameter budget.
- Same population size and selection implementation.
- Same stopping generation/game budget.
- Same validation schedule.
- Same fixed test suite.
- Recorded wall time, search nodes, leaf evaluations, and network operations.

Seeds:

- Five independent training seeds is the minimum defensible primary experiment.
- Ten seeds is preferable if corrected pilot variance is high.
- Use paired seed labels and common opening schedules across conditions.
- Do not treat games within one trained seed as independent training replicates.

Opening split:

- Training openings: diverse and resampled or drawn from a fixed training pool.
- Validation openings: frozen and never used for fitness.
- Test openings: frozen, hidden from checkpoint selection, and evaluated once.
- Every evaluation opening must be played twice with colors swapped.
- Prefer a recognized American Checkers opening protocol if licensing/data access permits; otherwise generate, validate, version, and publish the suite.

Checkpoint policy:

- Save predetermined generations, for example 0, 25, 50, 100, 150, and 200.
- Use validation performance to select one checkpoint per seed.
- Lock all selections before revealing test results.
- Preserve final-generation results too, avoiding sole reliance on selected maxima.

Primary endpoint:

- Held-out strict score against the fixed test opponent panel at the locked common search budget.

Secondary endpoints:

- Joint internal performance rating with bootstrap interval.
- Per-opponent W/D/L.
- Area under the validation-strength versus training-games curve.
- Games required to reach predefined strength thresholds.
- Robustness across unseen openings.
- Strength across search budgets.
- Active nodes, enabled connections, and parameter count.
- Search nodes and wall-clock cost.
- Frequency of disconnected, constant, cyclic, or invalid phenotypes.

Statistical analysis:

- Compute one primary result per training seed.
- Compare conditions using paired seed-level differences.
- Report mean, standard deviation, median, individual seed values, and 95 percent intervals.
- Use a hierarchical bootstrap that resamples training seeds first and opening pairs second.
- Report raw effect difference and a paired standardized effect size.
- Avoid claiming significance from game-level sample size.
- If testing several secondary endpoints, use Holm correction or clearly mark analyses exploratory.
- Treat learning-curve AUC as one prespecified endpoint rather than testing every generation.
- Include all failed/extinct runs under a predefined failure policy.

Paper reporting:

- Exact source commit and dirty-state check.
- Python and dependency lock file.
- Full NEAT configuration, including defaults.
- Hardware and process count.
- Total games, search nodes, network operations, CPU/wall time.
- Opening IDs and color assignments.
- Raw game records.
- Seed list.
- Checkpoint-selection policy.
- Exclusions and failures.
- Per-seed outcomes.
- Confidence intervals and effect sizes.
- Internal versus external rating distinction.
